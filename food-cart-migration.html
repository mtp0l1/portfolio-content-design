Moving Food onto the marketplace cart

Food had its own cart, and it was migrating to the marketplace cart. I went through every cart behavior and decided what Food could reuse and what it actually needed. Of 17 use cases, 12 reuse a capability that already exists and only one is new.

Problem

Food had three behaviors the marketplace cart didn’t

Most of a cart is shared behavior: pick items, change quantity, remove, show discounts and free shipping. Food adds three of its own: kits with choices set on the product page (a combo with a side and a drink, for example), a minimum purchase per store, and a service fee in the price box.

The handoff had to tell engineering, behavior by behavior, what to build, what to reuse as it is and what to leave alone.

Approach

I gave every use case one of four labels

I split the cart into 17 use cases. The label on each one answers the question engineering asks first: build it, reuse it or leave it?

Decisions

Where Food needed more than a reuse

Three decisions show the reasoning. The other cases follow the marketplace cart or what Food does today.

Decision 1

One rule covers three shipping setups

Meli+ free shipping and a store’s minimum purchase are separate thresholds, and either one can be the higher. That looked like three different flows. One sentence covers all of them.

The rule: only the minimum purchase decides whether the buyer can continue. Free shipping only changes what shipping costs.

What I left out. Free shipping never unlocks the checkout on its own.

Decision 2

The same kit twice: one row or two

Adding the same item again raises one question: is it the same customization? If it is different, the cart keeps two separate rows, as Food does today. If it is the same, the two collapse into one row with the quantity, “2 u.”

What I left out. Only identical kits merge. It is the one new behavior in the whole handoff, so I called it out instead of leaving it among the reused cases.

Decision 3

Editing a kit: back discards, confirm applies

From a kit’s row, the buyer reopens the product page with the choices already loaded. The variants link that the marketplace cart already has is the entry point, so the row gets nothing new.

What I left out. Going back never changes the cart. Only confirming on the product page applies the new choices.

Result

What the handoff made clear

Scope you can see

Labeling the 17 cases made the scope visible. Twelve reuse a capability that already exists, so the design effort went to the few places where Food differs.

One new thing, named

Only one case adds a behavior: identical kits share a row. Calling it out keeps it from getting lost among the reused ones.

One rule, not three flows

Free shipping and minimum purchase looked like three different flows. One sentence covers all three: the minimum decides whether the buyer can continue, and free shipping only changes the price of shipping.
