Pronto App for instant delivery

Four changes to the picker app so the supermarket can deliver in minutes.

Product and Content DesignerMercado Libre

Context

Pronto App is the app used by the people who pick supermarket orders. With instant delivery, the picker flow showed four gaps: volume that doesn't fit one courier, stock that triggers needless substitutions, bags with no count, and no proof of what was collected. Each one became its own handoff project.

What changed

  • A large order warns before collection, and the picker decides whether to call a second courier.
  • Low-stock items get a “Stock” tag. The picker offers a substitute instead of searching for more.
  • The picker confirms the bag count and hands the order to the driver at the end of picking.
  • At the store exit, the Customs screen shows what was collected, even if the supermarket's system is down.

What I did

  • Read the picker flow end to end and separated what broke with delivery in minutes.
  • Designed and wrote a screen or a notice for each point, with its own handoff.
  • Defined what the app tells the picker and what the picker decides, without adding a step.

Results

  • 4 fronts designed and documented for engineering, each tackling one gap in the flow.
  • Volume alert from 15 units, with no extra time added to picking.

Food benefit at Súper

Paying for groceries with the food benefit, right inside Mercado Libre.

Product and Content DesignerMercado Libre

Context

People who receive their food benefit through Mercado Pago can use it on Súper purchases. The opportunity was to grow the vertical's sales by showing the benefit on the store home and at payment. The first version is for Mercado Libre Brazil employees.

What changed

  • The store home shows the benefit balance next to free shipping and delivery.
  • At checkout, the benefit appears among the recommended payment methods, with the balance.
  • After the purchase, the detail shows how much was paid with the benefit.

What I did

  • Mapped where the benefit needs to show up, from the store home to the purchase detail.
  • Treated the benefit as a new payment method, with a balance and a “New” tag.
  • Documented the use cases, specs and translations.

Results

  • 3 points of the flow designed: store home, checkout and purchase detail.
  • First version handed off to engineering, with use cases, specs and translations.

Pausing a store in the backoffice

A controlled way to stop incoming orders when a store can't keep up.

Product and Content DesignerMercado Libre

Context

A store runs normally, but at times it gets more orders than it can process. There was no reliable rule to hold back demand, and the queue kept growing until operations broke down. The MVP combines two answers: automatic pausing by capacity and manual pausing for contingencies. This case is the manual flow, in the backoffice, for the Mercado Libre team leader.

What changed

  • The team leader picks until when to pause and why, in a short modal.
  • The modal says orders in progress aren't affected. Only new ones are paused.
  • The store screen shows who paused it, until when and why, with a button to resume.
  • The store list shows the Paused status and the time it comes back.

What I did

  • Designed the pause and resume flow in the backoffice, restricted to people with a specific permission.
  • Wrote the copy to make clear this is an exception tool, not an everyday one.
  • Documented the use cases, the errors and the automatic resume.

Results

  • 1 flow to pause and resume a store, with a reason and a record of who did it.
  • Handoff with 15 use cases, including date and time errors, failures and expired sessions.

Complaints for Super Instore and Darkstore

The supermarket's complaint trees, reorganized so buyers can describe the problem in a few steps.

Product and Content DesignerMercado Libre

Context

When something goes wrong with a supermarket order, the buyer opens a complaint and picks the problem from a tree of options. The trees were outdated: fields that no longer exist, no message when a complaint ends without compensation, and a different tone in each vertical. They will also become the rule base for the AI assistant that handles complaints.

What changed

  • The buyer picks the problem in a few questions, with direct options like “Arrived damaged or open”.
  • A complaint that ends without compensation now gets a closing message, instead of a dead end.
  • People who need help say what happened and attach photos of the package and the product.
  • After sending, the screen offers to continue the conversation on WhatsApp.

What I did

  • Reviewed the Instore and Darkstore complaint trees and removed what no longer exists.
  • Aligned the copy with Food, in a tone that acknowledges the buyer without admitting fault.
  • Documented each path, like a product with a problem or a package that didn't arrive, and where each exit goes.

Results

  • Instore and Darkstore trees updated, with the obsolete fields removed.
  • Copy aligned with Food and ready to be the rule base for the AI assistant.

Car insurance quote at Porto

A quote journey people can understand without needing a broker.

Product and Content DesignerPorto

Context

People who wanted car insurance at Porto couldn't get a full quote on their own and needed a broker. The company wanted to give customers autonomy, and the call center kept getting calls from people who didn't understand the insurance information. A content audit, a heuristic review and customer interviews confirmed it: the problem was the language.

What changed

  • The home promises “insurance that's easy to understand” and shows the benefits in three cards.
  • Buying is laid out in three steps, with the inspection done from home.
  • Coverages and assistance get icons and a short explanation, instead of insurance jargon.
  • Each quote question explains why it exists, like why we ask for the license plate.

What I did

  • Did the content audit and the heuristic review with the team, and listened to customers about each step of the quote.
  • With the product designer, built the storytelling, the hierarchy and the information architecture.
  • Wrote the new quote journey, with clear microcopy in Porto's tone of voice.

Results

  • 75% of the texts were easy to read for 6th to 9th grade level.
  • In the test, 4 of 6 people said they understood the insurance better, and 5 found the steps comfortable.
