Car insurance without needing a broker

Clear text instead of insurance jargon, so people understand what they buy and can close on their own.

Product and Content DesignerPorto

Context

People who wanted car insurance at Porto depended on a broker to close the sale, because the product had no self-service. The company wanted to give people autonomy, and the call center kept getting calls from people who didn't understand the insurance information. A content audit, a heuristic review and many customer interviews showed it: people were afraid of buying online, thought there would be too many steps, and insurance jargon made everything harder to understand.

Results

84% of the texts were easy to read for 6th to 9th grade level, in the readability calculator

In the quantitative survey, 8 of 8 people said they understood the insurance information better, and 7 of 8 found the flow simpler and more practical

What I did

Joined at the start of the workflow, in desk research, benchmarking and the heuristic content review, and listened to customers about each step of the quote.

From the How Might We, raised the UX Writing initiatives and organized them in a long-term activity map.

With the product designer, built the storytelling, the hierarchy and the information architecture.

Wrote the new quote journey, with zero states and error, denial and confirmation messages, in Porto's tone of voice.

What changed

The home promises “insurance that's easy to understand” and shows the benefits in three cards.

“Buy online, no obstacles” sums up buying in three steps, with the inspection done from home.

“What does our insurance cover?” shows each coverage with an icon and a short name, instead of insurance jargon.

“Why not more discounts?” gathers the perks from the Trânsito mais gentil app, Vivo and Itaú.

Each quote question explains why it exists, like why we ask for the license plate.
