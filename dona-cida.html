Dona Cida’s first sale

A 40-second story about a first-time seller reaching her first sale, and a test of one question: can a content designer direct the whole process with generative AI, from story and character to scenes, voice and quality review?

Conceptual piece, made independently. Not an official Mercado Libre campaign.

Audio in Brazilian Portuguese. The full script with English translation is in the Script section.

Audience and story

Why Dona Cida

Dona Cida is a woman in her 60s who has always made crochet for her family and decides to start selling it.

I chose an older, first-time seller with a handmade product on purpose. If she can start, anyone can, and handmade goods are very typical of the sellers who join Mercado Libre. The story had to carry that message without saying it out loud.

The journey is cut into four moments, each one a step a new seller really goes through. Each moment is its own short clip, which also made the production easier to control.

1

The decision

She has always made crochet for the family. Today she decides to sell.

2

The first step

She photographs the crochet bag to list it.

3

The first sale

The notification arrives, and so does the surprise.

4

The handover

She hands the package to the courier and closes the story.

Script

Short lines, warm tone, no hard sell

The tone is colloquial and close, like someone telling a neighbor about her week. The closing line states the benefit without selling anything.

My role

AI generated. I directed and decided.

I defined the goal and the story, directed the AI tools and made every quality decision. Each tool had a clear job.

Claude

Drafted the script and the scene prompts from my brief. I edited both before anything went to video.

Google Veo in Flow

Generated video and voice. A character reference kept Dona Cida consistent across scenes.

Me

Reviewed every generation, wrote the feedback for the next prompt and decided what to keep, fix or discard.

Evaluation

Five criteria, three possible decisions

I judged each generation against explicit criteria, then made one of three calls for every clip: keep it, fix it with a new prompt, or discard it.

CriterionWhat I checked
Character consistencySame face, hair and clothing from one clip to the next.
Believable actionsHands, objects and the direction of each action behave like real life.
Natural emotionHer expression changes with the moment instead of staying fixed.
Tone of voiceWarm and colloquial, matching the way the lines were written.
Closing line clarityThe final line is complete, audible and understood on first viewing.

Three versions to get it right

Every problem below was caught in review, traced to a criterion and fixed by changing the brief, not by generating again and hoping. Discarded clips stayed in the library for reference.

What every scene prompt had to specify by version 3

The fixes turned into a checklist. Each one closes a gap the model filled on its own in earlier versions.

1

Who does what to whom. The action and its direction, stated explicitly.

2

How many people are in the scene. Every character counted.

3

Camera and object handling. The angle, and how hands and objects interact.

4

The emotion for this moment. One expression per beat, not one for the whole film.

5

Continuity. Character reference and the same clothing in every clip.

What I learned

The prompt is the content brief

Specific actions mean fewer errors

In AI video, the prompt does the job of a content brief. The more specific the action, the fewer the errors in the output.

Human review is the quality control

Models don’t catch logical errors, like who delivers to whom, or problems of tone. Review against clear criteria is what controls quality.

Short scenes are easier to fix

Splitting the story into short clips made it possible to fix one part without redoing everything.

Next steps

From one video to a personalized system

The script already has a fixed structure: decision, first step, first sale, handover, closing line. That makes it a template.

Personalized versions
Keep the same four moments and swap the variable lines for other seller profiles, like a student selling a used instrument or a shop owner expanding to other states.
Test with real sellers
Check comprehension and tone with real new sellers before using the video anywhere.
