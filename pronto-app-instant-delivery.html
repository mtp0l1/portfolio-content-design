Pronto App: order picking for deliveries in minutes

Volume, stock, bags and proof of what was collected: four flow failures, four workstreams.

Product DesignerMercado Libre

Problem

Pronto App is the app used by the people who pick supermarket orders. With instant delivery, its flow showed four failures.

  1. Volumean order that doesn’t fit one courier
  2. Stocklow stock that triggers needless substitutions
  3. Bagsbags with no count
  4. Proofno proof of what was collected

Each one became its own handoff project.

How I found them

Led on-site discovery with operations using Shadowing (unobtrusive field observation) and intercept interviews with pickers and store leaders, combined with quantitative telemetry analysis of delivery errors to isolate the four critical flow failures.

Decision

Inline cues instead of blocking modals. The volume alert (15+ items) and the “Stock” tag live on the screen itself, with no extra step. Testing showed blocking modals slowed picking down.

Why I decided this way

  • Volume (15+ units)Splitting the order automatically would pay for a second courier for nothing on light items. Warning before collection and letting the picker decide protects the SLA without blowing up logistics cost.
  • StockHunting for an out-of-stock item blows the SLA, and substituting without notice breaks the buyer's trust. The tag avoids the pointless search, and the substitution only goes ahead after a clear confirmation.
  • Offline customs checkThe receipt is saved locally in the app. That way, leaving the store does not depend on the supermarket system's instability.

How I tested it

Validated the interface through usability testing, combining 5-Second Tests (quick alert scanning), First-Click Testing (first-tap accuracy), and one-handed mobile tests on the move. Designed and wrote a dedicated screen or alert for each failure point, with its own handoff and no extra steps.

My role

I ran the field discovery, designed the decision architecture and wrote the UI and copy for the four tracks, and ran the tests.

I worked in partnership with the PM and engineering, who defined the volume cutoff rule and the local persistence of the customs check.

The Volume track caused the most friction with PM and engineering: splitting the order automatically looked safer for the SLA, but it paid for a second courier for nothing.

The agreement was to warn before collection and let the picker decide, with 15 units as the trigger for the notice.

Results

4 tracks prioritized from real failures in the operation, each with its own handoff to engineering

Delivery-in-minutes SLA protected: the volume notice and the “Stock” tag show up before collection with no extra step, avoiding the delay blocking modals caused in picking

Fewer delays at the store door and less unnecessary logistics cost: the customs check works offline, and the picker only calls a second courier when the order really needs one

Reading and touch validated on the move: contrast, hierarchy and touch-area adjustments made after the tests ensured quick recognition of the notices and an accurate first tap with one hand
